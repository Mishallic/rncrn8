# Review: clinic-newsletter-plan.md

Problems that matter, most important first.

1. **The only acquisition channel is a cold blast to a bought list, which is legally restricted and will likely wreck deliverability.** *(How we reach them)*
   Under PECR, unsolicited marketing email to "individual subscribers" (sole traders and partnerships) needs prior consent, and many independent dental practices are sole traders or partnerships. Emails tied to named owners are also personal data under UK GDPR, so you need a lawful basis and must tell people where you got their address. Most email platforms ban purchased lists outright, and 12,000 cold sends from a new domain in one week is a classic way to get the account suspended and the domain blacklisted. That blocks the paid launch in week 2.
   **Fix:** Before buying, get written proof from the broker of how consent was collected. Split the list into limited companies (cold B2B email allowed with clear identification and an opt-out) and sole traders/partnerships (consent only). Write a legitimate-interests assessment. Send from a warmed-up domain in small, staggered batches through a platform that allows the data. Add opt-in channels that don't depend on the list: dental associations and local dental committees, trade press, supplier partnerships, conferences, LinkedIn, and referrals.

2. **The plan's own funnel produces 240 subscribers at best, not 600.** *(Who buys it / How we reach them)*
   20% open × 10% subscribe = 2% of the list. Even if the list covered all 12,000 practices, that is 240, less than half of the 600 (5%) the revenue figure relies on. It is also a one-off send, and the plan has no channel for winning subscribers after week 1. A 20% open rate on a cold purchased list is optimistic, and open rates are unreliable now anyway because Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates them.
   **Fix:** Work out the subscriber target from the funnel, not from a top-down percentage. Use the real list size, a cold-list open rate and a conversion rate, and add the recurring channels from item 1 with their own expected numbers. Before committing, test on a few hundred contacts, measuring clicks and paid sign-ups rather than opens. Offer a free trial of 2 to 4 issues instead of asking for payment after a single sample.

3. **The £208,800 revenue figure is overstated several ways.** *(Who buys it)*
   It assumes all 600 subscribers pay monthly for the full 12 months from day one. It ignores the ramp-up, the annual plan (£290 is £58 less than 12 × £29), churn and VAT. At £208,800 you are far above the £90,000 VAT registration threshold, so if £29 includes VAT, only £24.17 of it is yours.
   **Fix:** Build a month-by-month model with new subscribers per month, an assumed monthly/annual split, monthly churn, and prices stated ex-VAT. Customers are businesses that can usually reclaim VAT, so quoting £29 + VAT is reasonable.

4. **The timeline cannot happen in the order written.** *(Timeline)*
   The writer is hired in week 1 and the sample issue, which that writer has to write, also goes out in week 1. The two-issue buffer in Risks can't exist by week 2 either. "Month 3: reach 600 subscribers" contradicts "5% in the first year" and the funnel's 240 maximum.
   **Fix:** Hire the writer 4 to 6 weeks before launch. Have the sample issue and at least 2 to 4 buffer issues ready before any send. Do the compliance and domain warm-up from item 1 before the first send. Replace the month-3 target with milestones taken from the model in items 2 and 3.

5. **The costs are incomplete, so break-even is understated.** *(Costs)*
   On the stated figures, break-even is 91 subscribers, not 90: £2,550 ÷ (£29 × 0.97 = £28.13) = 90.65. With prices including VAT it rises to about 109. If subscribers take the annual plan it rises further, to about 131 for annual subscribers paying VAT-inclusive prices. Missing costs:
   - the data list itself
   - an email platform for 12,000 contacts, which is likely well over £50/month
   - employer costs if the writer is an employee
   - accounting and legal or data-protection advice
   - professional indemnity insurance, since clinics will act on the regulatory content
   - any paid marketing
   - your own time
   
   **Fix:** List every cost, price ex-VAT, and recalculate break-even with the expected monthly/annual mix. Include the one-off launch costs and work out how many months it takes to pay them back.

6. **The market size counts the wrong population.** *(Who buys it)*
   "About 12,000 dental practices" is all UK practices, but the product is for owners of independent clinics. Practices owned by corporate groups are not buyers, and an owner with several practices buys one subscription. So the market is smaller than 12,000, and the 5% is a percentage of the wrong number.
   **Fix:** Estimate the number of independent owners (not practices). Check how many of them the broker's list actually reaches and use that number in the funnel.

7. **The Risks section leaves out the main risks, and its one mitigation is weak.** *(Risks)*
   The real main risks are:
   - low conversion
   - the list being unusable (item 1)
   - churn after the novelty wears off
   - liability if a regulatory summary is wrong and a clinic acts on it
   
   For the writer leaving, two pre-written issues cover only two weeks. They will also go stale, because regulatory and price news is time-sensitive.
   **Fix:** Add each risk with a mitigation and a stop/continue threshold, for example "if fewer than N paid after the trial cohort, stop". Keep evergreen backup pieces rather than dated issues. Line up a second contributor or editor, and add a fact-check step and a disclaimer for the regulatory content.

8. **"UK" covers four different regulatory systems.** *(What we will sell)*
   Dental regulation and NHS dental contracts differ between England (CQC), Scotland (HIS), Wales (HIW) and Northern Ireland (RQIA). One page a week covering all four will be thin for each reader.
   **Fix:** Launch for England only, the largest group, or tag sections by nation. Then decide whether the other nations justify separate editions.
